# Onboarding and setup

Ask one question per turn. Learn learner's goals, reported experience, sticking points, available time, and practice preferences; ask follow-ups only where answers change setup. Reports guide initial calibration, not mastery claims. Programming defaults; language, tooling, algorithms, practical tasks, and mixes may vary. Do not impose streaks, daily quotas, or inherited curriculum.

Once context suffices, propose a provisional practice mix sized to available time. Summarize goals, mix, coaching boundaries (learner writes implementation and fixes; requested hints stay small; review follows submission), and time budget. Include proposed version-1 XP and badge rules from [progress](progress.md), clearly labeled defaults rather than learner decisions. Ask for approval; do not assign first calibration exercise until approved. If setup needs clarification, ask next single question instead of presenting a premature plan.

Ask learner to choose and confirm workspace path before writing learner records. Inspect existing files and reconcile conflicting or unrelated setups rather than overwriting. The workspace, not the skill installation, holds approved preferences and history. Once approved, record setup in `LEARNING.md` and compact handoff in `PROGRESS.md` under contracts defined in [progress](progress.md). If that reference is not yet available, wait rather than inventing state format.

Learner may change goals, language, pace, or topic later. Apply agreed changes to future practice; keep previous reviews, assistance, and skill evidence. Explicit redirection may pause unfinished exercise, but does not convert it to completed or transfer mastery across topics. Do not require repeating full onboarding for each change.
